## Executive Summary

**Platform builds sustainable expert ecosystem through realistic revenue sharing:**

- **Expert Marketplace Commission: %10-15 tiered** (vs. initial %25)
  - Attracts quality experts with higher earnings
  - Scales expert network to 300-500 active consultants
  - Enables sustainable marketplace network effects

- **Agricultural Product B2B Commission: %5** (vs. initial %3)
  - Reflects higher platform coordination value
  - Aligns incentives: farmers earn more, companies save more, platform captures fair share
  - Sustainable at high volume (20,000+ tons by Year 3)

**Key Principle:** *Revenue sharing should create win-win-win: experts earn well, farmers benefit, companies save, platform sustains operations and growth.*

## 1. Expert Marketplace: Tiered Commission Model

### 1.1 Why Tiering Matters

**Problem with flat %25 commission:**
- Too high for new/verified experts (not enough income)
- Discourages expert retention (high churn expected)
- Reduces attractiveness vs. competitors (Upwork, Fiverr average 15-20%)
- Limits platform scale (small expert network = limited value)

**Solution with tiered %10-15:**
- Incentivizes quality experts to join and stay
- Creates achievement ladder (new → verified → featured)
- Balances platform economics with expert earnings
- Enables 300-500 expert network by Year 3

### 1.2 Commission Tier Structure

```

Requirements:
├─ Recently joined (< 30 days)
├─ Basic credentials verified
├─ No ratings yet
└─ Can accept up to 5 projects/month initially

├─ Platform invests in onboarding & vetting

├─ Annual: ~150K TL (sustainable side income for agronomist)
└─ Incentive: Strong (comparable to Upwork experts)
```

## 2. B2B Agricultural Product Sourcing: %5 Commission

### 2.1 Rationale for %5 Commission

**Why %5 (not %3):**

```
PLATFORM VALUE CREATION IN B2B SOURCING:
├─ Farmer Verification: Credentials, quality standards, batch consistency
├─ Batch Coordination: Aggregating multiple farmers, logistics
├─ Quality Assurance: Inspection, testing, food safety compliance
├─ Contract Management: Smart contracts, payment escrow, dispute resolution
├─ Logistics: Last-mile coordination, cold chain if needed
├─ Regulatory: Compliance documentation, traceability, certifications
└─ Relationship Management: B2B buyer support, farmer communication

Cost to Platform:
├─ Vetting & onboarding: ~5K TL per food company, 1K TL per farmer batch
├─ Quality testing: 500 TL per batch (lab analysis, sampling)
├─ Logistics coordination: 1-2% of transaction value (minor, but real)
├─ Disputes & chargebacks: 0.5% of transaction value (insurance against defaults)
├─ Customer support: 0.5% of transaction value (buyer + seller support)
└─ Total OpEx: ~3-4% of transaction value

At %3 commission: Platform margin = -1% to 0% (unsustainable)
At %5 commission: Platform margin = 1-2% after OpEx (sustainable, allows growth investment)
```

**Comparison to alternatives:**

```
Traditional wholesale supply chain:
├─ Wholesaler margin: 40% (huge, this is what farmer loses)
└─ Farmer receives: 60% of end price

IA Direct B2B Model (%5):
├─ Platform takes: 5%
├─ Farmer receives: 95% of negotiated price
├─ Company pays: 5% premium vs. farmer wholesale price
│  (but still saves 35% vs. traditional wholesale)
└─ Everyone wins: Farmer +25%, Company -10%, Platform +5%
```

### 2.2 B2B Commission by Product Category

**Option: Tiered by product complexity/spoilage risk**

```
COMMODITY CROPS (wheat, corn, cotton):
├─ Commission: 3-4% (high volume, low complexity)
├─ Reason: Lower coordination cost, standardized
├─ Volume: 60% of B2B transactions
└─ Example: 1000 tons wheat @ 12 TL/kg = 12M TL GMV → 360-480K TL commission

HIGH-VALUE / PERISHABLE (vegetables, fruit, organic):
├─ Commission: 5-6% (coordination, cold chain, quality critical)
├─ Reason: Quality assurance, logistics, food safety
├─ Volume: 40% of B2B transactions
├─ Example: 500 tons tomato @ 22 TL/kg = 11M TL GMV → 550-660K TL commission

BLENDED COMMISSION (Year 2 onward):
├─ Weighted average: 60% × 3.5% + 40% × 5.5% ≈ 4.3%
├─ Growth: As premium products scale, blended rate trends toward 4.5-5%
└─ Year 3 assumption: 5% blended
```

**For simplicity, base model uses %5 blended across all categories.**

### 2.3 B2B Transaction Economics (Detailed)

```
SCENARIO: Food Company (Ülker, Doğadan) Direct Sourcing

Traditional Model (Wholesaler):
├─ Farmer production: 100 tons
├─ Wholesale price (farmer gets): 20 TL/kg = 2M TL
├─ Wholesaler markup: 40% = adds 800K TL
├─ Company pays: 2.8M TL for 100 tons
├─ Farmer earnings: 2M TL

IA Direct B2B Model (%5):
├─ Farmer production: 100 tons
├─ Negotiated price (farmer-company): 25 TL/kg = 2.5M TL
├─ Platform fee: 5% = 125K TL
├─ Company pays: 2.625M TL for 100 tons
│  (saves 175K TL vs. wholesaler = -6.7%)
├─ Farmer gets: 2.375M TL after platform fee
│  (earns +375K TL vs. wholesale = +18.75%)
└─ Platform gets: 125K TL

Everyone's economics:
├─ Farmer: +18.75% income (major incentive)
├─ Company: -6.7% cost (still saves 175K vs. traditional)
├─ Platform: 125K TL per 100-ton batch (5% commission)

Platform net after OpEx:
├─ Revenue: 125K TL
├─ OpEx (quality, logistics, support): ~50K TL (estimated 0.2% per batch)
├─ Platform net profit: 75K TL (60% margin)

Volume scaling:
├─ Year 1: 40 batches × 125K = 5M TL GMV → 500K TL commission → 300K profit
├─ Year 2: 100+ batches × 125K = 12.5M TL GMV → 1.25M TL commission → 750K profit
├─ Year 3: 240 batches × 125K = 30M TL GMV → 3M TL commission → 1.8M profit
```
